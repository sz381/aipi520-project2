Label validation

Do the labels mean what we think?

Completed trials pass about twice as often as trials stopped early, at every gate.

Agrees with an expert benchmark: when we say a Phase 2 passed, the TOP benchmark (HINT paper) says the trial succeeded 77% of the time (1,536 shared trials).

Spot checks: semaglutide, secukinumab, vericiguat → approved; evacetrapib, solanezumab → not.

Known limit: labels are drug-level. A drug that fails in one disease but advances in another counts as “passed”.

- Sanity check 1: if the label is meaningful, completed trials should pass more often than terminated ones. They do, roughly 2x at every gate. - Sanity check 2: compared with the TOP benchmark labels from the HINT paper. When we say passed, TOP agrees 77%. When we say failed, TOP still says success about half the time: meeting an endpoint does not guarantee the company continues. - Sanity check 3: well-known drugs land on the right side. - Limitation to state openly: the label is about the drug, not the single trial.
