Objective and scope

One model per gate of a new drug’s journey

Gate 1

Phase 1 → Phase 2

Input: an industry Phase 1 trial of a drug never tested in Phase 2

Who asks: early-stage investors

Gate 2

Phase 2 → Phase 3

Input: an industry Phase 2 trial of a drug never tested in Phase 3

Who asks: acquirers, who usually buy before Phase 3

Gate 3

Phase 3 → FDA approval

Input: an industry Phase 3 trial (US site) of a drug not yet approved

Who asks: sponsors planning a launch

Scope choices: industry sponsors only (academic trials rarely continue even when they succeed) · new drugs only (mature drugs make the task artificially easy) · trials started 2005–2019 (every trial has years to show its outcome)

- The assignment: input one trial, output a probability of success. We define success as passing the next gate. - Why three gates: they are exactly the three labelling ideas from the lecture (Phase 2 exists, Phase 3 exists, FDA approval). - Why industry only: academic Phase 2s reached Phase 3 only 10% of the time vs 22% for industry; the label means something different there. - Why new drugs only: drugs already in Phase 3 elsewhere almost always get another one; including them inflates AUC. - Why 2005–2019: each trial needs at least 6 years to show whether it moved on.
