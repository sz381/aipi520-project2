Interpretation

What we learned

Later gates are easier to call

0.72 → 0.72 → 0.81: by Phase 3 a drug has years of history, trials are standardised, and FDA approval is a cleaner label.

Companies signal conviction

More recent trials, more endpoints and more indications all raise the odds. A sponsor’s past track record does not.

Public data has a ceiling

Three algorithms agree. Going higher needs molecular, target and omics data, as InSilico uses.

Limitations

Labels are drug-level, not trial-level · FDA approval covers the US only · registry records get edited after the fact · the Phase 3 test set is only 812 trials

- Why later gates are easier: more accumulated history per drug, more standardised confirmatory trials, and an official approval label instead of "a later trial exists". - The strongest signals are about company commitment, not trial design details. - With public registry data alone, about 0.72 to 0.81 is what we can defend; reaching InSilico-level 0.88 needs proprietary drug data. - Limitations, said plainly: drug-level labels, US-only approvals, records edited after the fact, smaller Phase 3 test set.
